# Director vocabulary

Options to choose from when planning a shot. Pick the one or two that serve the intent; a prompt that lists many
of these at once directs nothing.

- Shot sizes and angles
- Composition

## Shot sizes and angles

Sizes: extreme wide, wide, medium wide, medium, medium close-up, close-up, extreme close-up, over-the-shoulder,
insert, establishing.

Angles: eye level, low angle, high angle, ground level, shoulder height, overhead, profile, three-quarter view.

## Composition

Rule of thirds, centred, symmetrical, leading lines, negative space, frame within a frame, foreground layering,
environmental framing.

In a prompt, state the result rather than the term: "framed through the doorway", "foreground objects create
depth", "she occupies the right third of the frame", "large empty space behind him".

## Lens feel

Lens words communicate intention; they are not an optical simulation.

| Lens | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 24mm | Strong environmental perspective | Landscapes, interiors, architecture, dramatic depth |
| 35mm | Natural cinematic perspective | Character in an environment, movement |
| 50mm | Balanced and human | General-purpose framing |
| 85mm | Compressed background, intimate | Emotional close-ups, portraits |
| Macro | Extreme detail | Eyes, textures, mechanisms, food, small objects |

Descriptive alternatives: shallow depth of field, deep focus, soft background separation, compressed background,
foreground blur, natural perspective, subtle lens distortion.

## Camera movement

Static, push-in, pull-back, tracking, dolly, pan, tilt, handheld, crane, orbit, pedestal, rack focus.

Give the move a reason and a speed:
- "The camera slowly pushes toward his face as he realizes what happened."
- "The camera tracks backward at the same speed as the running subject."
- "A slow pan reveals the abandoned city."
- "The shot stays completely static, which holds the tension."

One primary move per shot. Orbit plus zoom plus pan plus track in one clip reads as noise.

## Performance details

Facial expression, eye direction, head movement, posture, body language, breathing, blinking, hesitation, weight
shifting, hand movement, reaction timing, walking rhythm, running stride, interaction with objects.

## Physics and motion

Momentum, weight, acceleration and deceleration, gravity, cloth and hair movement, foot contact, surface
interaction, wind response, collisions, water behaviour, dust, debris, rain interaction, vehicle suspension,
inertia.

Examples:
- "His coat trails behind him as he accelerates, pulled by momentum and wind."
- "Her feet keep solid contact with the wet pavement while her weight shifts through the turn."

Avoid impossible or contradictory motion unless the user asks for stylized or supernatural behaviour.

## Lighting sources

Name where the light comes from:
- warm tungsten light from a nearby storefront
- moonlight through a window
- soft overcast daylight
- flashing emergency lights
- neon signs reflecting off wet pavement
- sunlight through tree branches
- a computer monitor lighting the face
- a single overhead fluorescent fixture

Light should reinforce the intent. Stacking many lighting terms cancels them out.

## Environment and materials

Weather, architecture, room layout, street conditions, fog, rain, smoke, dust, surface texture, metal, glass,
wood, fabric, wet pavement, reflections, atmospheric haze.

Describe materials doing something: "Rainwater collects in shallow puddles that reflect the neon signs."

## Continuity checklist

Character identity, face, hair, clothing, accessories, body proportions, environment, location, weather, time of
day, lighting direction, screen direction, eye lines, props, vehicle appearance, colour palette.
